# Business case

Your prototype shows *what* the product looks like. This document explains *why* it could become a business. Answer in your own words and refer to course concepts by name. See the term project brief on Moodle for length and grading.

## 1. The idea

My customers are newcomers to a town. Anyone arriving for the first time, such as students, families and travelers. Local Town Hub is a website where they can find what they need quickly in one place. A parent can find a school or daycare for their children, where it is and its phone number. They can also find restaurants, hospitals, services, gyms, attractions and hikes.

Today, newcomers need to piece these things together from Google, Facebook groups, tours and asking people they meet. When I came to Antigonish, I did the same. Even though my cousin was here, I still struggled, because my needs were different from his and I kept finding things he did not know about. It took time to understand the town. Having everything in one place makes life easier, and people keep coming back to what is easy and convenient.

I am starting with Antigonish as a sample. Local Town Hub website would make money in three ways: local businesses pay to be featured, advertisers pay for ads, and town councils pay us to build and run a hub for their town. Later, the buy and sell section can earn money too, for example from paid listings.


## 2. Where IT creates value

The main value chain activity Local Town Hub changes is marketing and sales for local businesses.

As of now many local businesses do not have a good way to advertise themselves to newcomers. I have been in Antigonish for two to three months, and I still keep discovering good places, like seafood restaurants and coffee shops, that I never heard about. They are there, but newcomers do not find them easily.

On the newcomer side, people jump between Google, Facebook groups, Kijiji and asking around. Each one only covers part of what they need. Buy and sell sites are only for buying and selling, so they will not help you find a clinic or a school. If I get sick, I still have to search somewhere else to find where to go.

Local Town Hub puts everything in one place, organized in categories that are easy to navigate. For businesses, this means they get listed or featured exactly where newcomers look first, so they reach new customers with less effort and cost than other advertising. For newcomers, it saves time and effort because they do not have to search a and go through many sources. Basic public information, like hikes and attractions, stays free, which keeps people coming back and gives businesses more people to reach.


## 3. Network effects

Local Town Hub is a two-sided network. One side is the people who post: local businesses, event organizers and locals selling items. The other side is the people who browse: newcomers looking for places, services, events and things to buy.
When more businesses and locals post, the site has more useful content, so it attracts more traffic to it. When more newcomers visit, businesses get more exposure and sellers in the buy and sell section get more buyers, so posting becomes more worth it. Each side makes the site more valuable for the other side. There is also a same-side effect: when more people rate restaurants, services and sellers, the reviews become more useful for everyone reading them.


The network is local. Someone moving to Antigonish wants everything for Antigonish at their fingertips: attractions, services, emergencies and hospitals. Posts from another town do not help them. So the site could be organized by province, but each town has its own section.


To solve the cold start, I will first add the main places myself using information that is already public, such as schools, hospitals, pharmacies, grocery stores, attractions and hikes, so the site is useful on day one. Then I will bring traffic through search engines, social media ads, posters around town, and emails to students. Once there are enough visitors, businesses will have a reason to post.

The Town meter page on my prototype shows how the site becomes more useful as more people post.


## 4. Can it be copied? (the moat question)

Yes, my website can be copied. We are living in the era of AI, where anyone can rebuild a website like mine in a short amount of time. So the website itself is not my advantage. What could protect me are the resources I build over time. 

I tested three of them using VRIN.
1. A local network of posters and users in each town. It is valuable because more posters bring more traffic, and people who come for attractions also discover the buy and sell section, restaurants and services. It is rare because the big sites have huge traffic, but it is scattered: no single site has a town's attractions, services, buy and sell and events in one place. It is hard to imitate because a rival can copy my site quickly, but not the brand and traffic I have built around it. There are substitutes, like map apps, Facebook and Kijiji, so it only partly passes this test. The difference is that newcomers have to use several of them to get what my hub gives them in one place.


2. Local ratings and reviews. Newcomers can rate restaurants, services and sellers, and I can add subcategories like coffee shops, grills, sushi, burgers and seafood. This is valuable because it helps people choose and gives them a reason to come back. Big review sites exist, but they do not rate local sellers, and a new rival would not have my reviews. This is the resource that gets stronger over time: the more people rate, the more reliable the scores become, and a rival cannot get years of Antigonish reviews in a weekend.


3. Partnerships with main local places like StFX. A partnership is valuable because StFX could send its international students and newcomers to the hub. I could offer partners their own page for newcomers at a fair price. But this resource is weaker: any competitor could also approach StFX, so it is not rare or hard to imitate unless I offer something better than others do.
What would a big rival need? A big map or social media company would need to build a similar hub and fill it with local content. For a small town like Antigonish, they might not bother. But my idea is meant to scale. Once Local Town Hub is popular in many towns and shows real traffic and revenue, a big company would be interested. A social media company could simply add a "town hub" section on top of its huge user base. This is my biggest weak spot, along with people's habits: they are used to well-known apps for maps, reviews and buy and sell. My answer is to grow the network and reviews fast in each town, and keep improving the site so it is easy to use and reliable.
